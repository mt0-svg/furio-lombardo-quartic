A new version of the paper of v1.0 (Zenodo record [10.5281/zenodo.23049095](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23049095)): the written proof now follows the route and the lemma cuts of the Lean proof. The Lean development, the code and the checks are those of v1.0.1. Every Lean line of a theorem, proposition, lemma or corollary names declarations that the formal proof of `FurioLombardo.conjecture_1_6` uses; the Lean line of a remark names results proved in Lean that it does not use. Each numbered statement has the hypotheses of its Lean declaration, no more and no fewer.

- Proposition 2.1: the Lean line names `FurioLombardo.M2.Proved.isPrincipalIdealRing_M1K21`, on the field of the descent. The proof of Lemma 2.4 uses that the class group has no element of order 2, as the Lean proof does.
- Lemma 2.2 is the identity for the explicit forms Q_1, Q_2, Q_3 and the element c_B of the repository, now introduced before it (`FurioLombardo.M1.bruin_O`).
- Lemma 2.3: its first claim holds for every primitive integer vector (`FurioLombardo.M1.not_all_mem`); F(a,b,c) = 0 is assumed only for the other claims. It states the parity of the valuations for Q_1 and Q_3 wherever they do not vanish, as `FurioLombardo.M1.mem_selmer_of_qO` does; Theorem 2.7 uses it in that form. Its proof that one of them is nonzero reads the points of the curve modulo 5 from the sieve certificate, as `FurioLombardo.M1.exists_qO_ne_zero` does.
- Lemma 2.4: the Lean line names the inputs of the spanning lemma, which the proof of `FurioLombardo.M1.selmer_int` supplies: Proposition 2.1, `gU_mem`, `rank_le`, `S_finite` and `S_ncard`.
- Lemma 2.5 is stated as `FurioLombardo.M1.sieve_good`: at a primitive integer vector on the curve, an exponent vector e such that Q_i G_e is a square wherever Q_i does not vanish (i = 1, 3) is one of eight explicit vectors. Lemma 2.6 is stated at such a vector: six of the eight vectors fail that condition, and for the other two, e_0 and e_1, the product of delta_k and G for e_k is a square (`FurioLombardo.M1.known_eq`); the classes of the q(P_i) come from the lifts x_0 to x_3.
- Theorem 2.7 is stated for a primitive integer vector on the curve and a form Q_1 or Q_3 that does not vanish at it (`FurioLombardo.M1.selmer_int`). The statement on the descent class of a rational point, the form of v1.0, is Remark 2.8 (`FurioLombardo.M1.selmerSet` and `FurioLombardo.M1.selmerSet_class`).
- Corollary 2.9 (2.8 in v1.0) is proved from Theorem 2.7 at a primitive vector, as `FurioLombardo.M1.descent_Mmat` is. Its Lean line names the lifts of the four known points by their definitions.
